## Design Context

### Users
**Primary audience:** Executives and engineering leadership (CTOs, VPs of Engineering, Directors). They access this dashboard to get a high-level read on engineering health — PR throughput, review velocity, developer output — and surface it in leadership reviews or 1:1s. They are time-constrained and need clarity at a glance, not exploration. They expect the tool to feel as premium as the decisions they're making with it.

### Brand Personality
**Precise · Trustworthy · Efficient**

This is a tool that handles sensitive team performance data. The interface must feel authoritative without being intimidating, and polished without being showy. Like a Bloomberg terminal that's been designed by someone who cares about craft.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Dark mode, sleek — inspired by Linear.**

- Full dark mode. Background: near-black (`#0a0a0f` or `#0d0d14`), surfaces: `#13131a` / `#1a1a24`
- Sidebar: deep dark, subtly distinct from main content
- Typography: Inter, tighter letter-spacing, confident weight hierarchy
- Accent color: a single cool-toned primary (indigo/violet range — e.g. `#6366f1` or `#818cf8`) used sparingly
- Data ink: white/off-white numbers with secondary grey labels
- Cards: subtle border (`1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07)`), no heavy shadows, slight transparency
- Charts: muted line fills, accent-colored active series
- NO: MUI default blue (#1976d2), pastel gradients, playful icons, cluttered chrome, Grafana-style panel walls

**Anti-references:** Jira, any colorful SaaS (Asana, Monday), generic MUI out-of-the-box, Grafana wall-of-panels

**Reference:** Linear — dark, typographically precise, every element earns its place

### Design Principles

1. **Data is the hero.** Numbers are large, labels are small. The metric value should be immediately readable from arm's length. Remove chrome that competes with data.

2. **Earn trust through restraint.** One accent color, used for interactive elements and active states only. No decorative color. No gradients unless they serve data (chart fills).

3. **Density without clutter.** Executive dashboards need information density, but not visual noise. Use spacing and subtle separators, not boxes-within-boxes. Whitespace is structure.

4. **Dark by design.** Don't adapt a light theme to dark — design for dark from the start. Surfaces layer with opacity and brightness, not with heavy borders or shadows.

5. **Typographic hierarchy does the work.** Bold numbers, medium labels, light secondary text. The eye should always know where to look next without being guided by color.
